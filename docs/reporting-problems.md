# Reporting FMG Problems

FMGs can encounter problems involving examinations, registration, internship, CRMI allocation, documentation, fees, stipends, or institutional procedures.

This guide provides a structured way to document and investigate such problems.

## Why Report a Problem?

A report can help identify:

- Repeated administrative problems
- Conflicting instructions
- Missing or unclear official information
- Institution-specific requirements
- Delays in registration or internship allocation
- Changes in procedures
- Potential gaps between published rules and practical implementation

A single report does not establish that a problem is universal.

Repeated reports supported by documentation may justify further investigation.

## Before Reporting

Try to collect the relevant information first.

Useful information includes:

- State
- Institution
- Type of process involved
- Approximate date
- Authority or institution involved
- What you were told
- What you expected based on the published rules
- What actually happened
- Relevant documents, notices, screenshots, or receipts
- Official sources you consulted

Do not publish unnecessary personal information.

## Do Not Publish Sensitive Information

Remove or obscure:

- Passport numbers
- Aadhaar numbers
- Medical registration numbers
- Application numbers
- Phone numbers
- Email addresses
- Home addresses
- Personal financial information
- Patient information
- Other identifying information that is not necessary to understand the issue

## Recommended Report Format

Use the following structure:

### Location

State:

### Institution / Authority

Name:

### Process

For example:

- FMGE application
- FMGE result
- Provisional registration
- CRMI allocation
- Internship
- Permanent registration
- Stipend
- Document verification

### Date

Approximate date of the event:

### What Happened?

Describe the event factually.

### What Were You Told?

Record the instruction you received as accurately as possible.

### What Does the Official Source Say?

Provide the relevant official source and, where possible, the exact section or page.

### Evidence

List supporting documents or screenshots.

### Current Status

For example:

- Resolved
- Awaiting response
- Escalated
- Still unresolved
- Information only

## Evidence Levels

Reports should be understood according to the evidence available.

### Level 1 — Personal report

A person reports that something happened.

Useful for identifying issues, but not sufficient to establish a general rule.

### Level 2 — Multiple independent reports

Several people report similar experiences.

This can indicate a recurring practical issue but still does not establish an official requirement.

### Level 3 — Documentary evidence

The report is supported by documents, screenshots, correspondence, or other records.

### Level 4 — Official confirmation

The relevant authority or institution has formally confirmed the information.

## Complaints vs Documentation

This project is primarily a documentation and information resource.

A report should not automatically be treated as proof of misconduct, illegality, discrimination, or regulatory violation.

Where such claims are made, the guide should distinguish:

- What the person reports
- What documents show
- What the official rules state
- What the relevant authority has confirmed

## Escalation

If an issue requires action from an authority, users should contact the relevant official body.

Depending on the issue, this may include:

- NMC
- NBEMS
- State Medical Council
- Directorate of Medical Education
- State health department
- Relevant medical institution

The guide can help identify the appropriate authority, but it does not replace the authority's complaint or appeal process.

## Privacy

When contributing a report, protect your own privacy and the privacy of others.

Do not upload documents containing unnecessary personal information.

If a document contains sensitive information, redact it before sharing.

## Principle

> Document the problem without overstating what the evidence proves.

The purpose of reporting is to make the system easier to understand and to identify information that needs verification or correction.
