> **Official blueprint allocation:** 33 marks  
> **Section:** Medicine & Allied Subjects

## Purpose

This page is the Medicine section of the FMGE Open Guide.

It is designed to connect the official FMGE subject blueprint with a structured topic-level study framework.

The official allocation of 33 marks comes from the NBEMS FMGE blueprint.

The topic priorities on this page are **not official NBEMS weightings**. They will be populated progressively as question-level evidence is collected.

## 2. Topic Framework

The Medicine syllabus is divided into major systems and clinical domains.

| Domain | Topics |
|---|---|
| Cardiology | Ischemic heart disease, heart failure, valvular disease, arrhythmias, hypertension, cardiomyopathies |
| Respiratory | Obstructive disease, restrictive disease, pneumonia, tuberculosis, pleural disease, respiratory failure |
| Gastroenterology | Liver disease, GI bleeding, inflammatory bowel disease, malabsorption, pancreatic disease |
| Neurology | Stroke, seizures, movement disorders, demyelination, neuromuscular disease, CNS infections |
| Nephrology | AKI, CKD, glomerular disease, nephrotic/nephritic syndromes, electrolyte disorders, acid-base disorders |
| Endocrinology | Diabetes, thyroid disease, adrenal disease, pituitary disease, calcium disorders |
| Rheumatology | Connective-tissue disease, inflammatory arthritis, vasculitis, crystal arthropathies |
| Infectious Diseases | Bacterial infections, viral infections, HIV, tropical infections, antimicrobial therapy |
| Hematology | Anemias, leukemias, lymphomas, bleeding disorders, transfusion medicine |
| Emergency Medicine | Shock, poisoning, acute respiratory failure, altered consciousness, emergency cardiovascular conditions |
| Dermatological Medicine | Common systemic manifestations of dermatological disease |
| Geriatric Medicine | Common geriatric syndromes and age-related disease |
| Genetics | Inherited disorders and clinically relevant genetic syndromes |

## 3. Topic Priority

No topic is currently assigned an evidence-based priority.

This is intentional.

The project will not label topics as "high-yield" merely because they are commonly mentioned in coaching material or online discussions.

Priority will be assigned progressively using documented evidence.

### Planned priority inputs

1. Official subject allocation
2. Number of documented questions
3. Number of independent observations
4. Recency of observations
5. Repetition across examination sessions
6. Reliability of the underlying source

## 6. How to Study Medicine

A practical study cycle is:

### Step 1 — Learn

Understand the underlying clinical concept.

### Step 2 — Solve

Answer questions without immediately checking the explanation.

### Step 3 — Diagnose the error

Classify mistakes as:

- Knowledge gap
- Misinterpretation
- Recall failure
- Calculation error
- Question-reading error
- Distractor error

### Step 4 — Record

Add important errors or concepts to the candidate's revision system.

### Step 5 — Revisit

Re-test weak areas during subsequent revision cycles.

---

## 7. Recommended Clinical Thinking Framework

For clinical Medicine questions, candidates should generally think in the following order:

**Presentation → Localization → Differential → Investigation → Interpretation → Diagnosis → Management**

Not every FMGE question requires the complete sequence, but this framework helps prevent isolated fact memorization.

## 9. Evidence Policy

The following distinctions must remain explicit:

**Official**

> NBEMS specifies Medicine as 33 marks.

**Observed**

> A particular topic has appeared repeatedly in documented examination evidence.

**Interpretation**

> The available evidence suggests that the topic deserves additional revision attention.

**Recommendation**

> The project recommends allocating additional study time to that topic.

These statements have different evidentiary status and should never be presented as interchangeable.
