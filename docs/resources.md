# Official Resources

This page collects primary sources that should be used to verify information in the FMGE Open Guide.

## How to use this page

The guide prioritises **primary sources** wherever possible.

That means information should preferably be verified against:

1. National Medical Commission (NMC)
2. National Board of Examinations in Medical Sciences (NBEMS)
3. State Medical Councils
4. Central or State Government departments
5. Official medical colleges and universities
6. Other competent regulatory authorities

Secondary sources such as Reddit, Telegram, YouTube, blogs, and news articles can be useful for understanding real-world experiences, but should not be treated as the final authority for regulatory requirements.

---

## National Medical Commission

**National Medical Commission (NMC)**

Primary regulatory authority for medical education and medical registration in India.

Official website:

> [NMC](https://www.nmc.org.in/)

Use the NMC website to verify current information relating to:

- Medical registration
- Foreign Medical Graduates
- Medical education regulations
- Internship requirements
- Foreign medical qualifications
- Professional conduct
- Medical colleges
- Regulatory notifications

## National Board of Examinations in Medical Sciences

**National Board of Examinations in Medical Sciences (NBEMS)**

Official website:

> [NBEMS](https://natboard.edu.in/)

Use NBEMS as the primary source for examination-related information, including:

- FMGE
- Information bulletins
- Application notices
- Examination dates
- Admit cards
- Results
- Candidate instructions
- Examination-related notices

## State Medical Councils

State Medical Councils are important sources for state-specific registration procedures.

The relevant council should be identified based on the candidate's circumstances.

State-specific pages in this guide should link directly to the appropriate council.

## Government Sources

Relevant information may also be published by:

- Ministry of Health and Family Welfare
- Government of India
- State Health Departments
- State governments
- Other competent government authorities

Official government notifications should be preferred over secondary summaries.

# Source Verification Policy

## 1. Prefer primary sources

Whenever a claim concerns a regulation, eligibility requirement, fee, deadline, registration requirement, or legal/administrative procedure, use the original official source whenever possible.

## 2. Record the date

Regulatory information can become outdated.

Important pages should therefore record:

**Last verified:** YYYY-MM-DD

## 3. Preserve historical information

When a rule changes, do not simply erase the previous information if it is historically useful.

Instead:

- Clearly mark the old information as historical.
- Add the date range for which it applied.
- Link to the newer rule.
- Preserve the original source where possible.

## 4. Distinguish fact from experience

A statement such as:

> "The State Medical Council requires X."

should have an official source.

A statement such as:

> "Several FMGs reported being asked for X."

should identify the experience as community-reported and, where possible, provide supporting evidence.

## 5. Avoid unsourced claims

If the source for an important claim cannot be established, mark the information as:

**Source needed**

rather than presenting it as established fact.

# Community Sources

Community sources can be valuable for documenting real-world experiences.

Examples include:

- FMG interviews
- Reddit discussions
- Public forums
- Personal accounts
- Videos
- Community-maintained documents

However, community reports should be clearly labelled as such.

They should **not** automatically be presented as evidence of an official requirement.
