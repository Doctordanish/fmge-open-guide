# Sources & Verification

FMGE Open Guide is intended to be a source-driven knowledge base.

Medical licensing requirements, examination procedures, internship allocations, registration rules, fees, and institutional requirements can change. Important information should therefore be traceable to a source and associated with a verification date.

## Source Hierarchy

When researching a requirement, use sources in approximately this order.

### 1. National Medical Commission

The NMC is the primary authority for many regulations governing medical education, foreign medical graduates, registration, and internship requirements.

Official website:

https://www.nmc.org.in/

### 2. National Board of Examinations in Medical Sciences

NBEMS is the primary source for examination-related information such as:

- FMGE information bulletins
- Application dates
- Examination dates
- Admit cards
- Results
- Pass certificates
- Examination notices

Official website:

https://natboard.edu.in/

### 3. State Medical Councils

State Medical Councils are important sources for state-level registration procedures and requirements.

Where a state-specific procedure is described, the relevant State Medical Council should be checked whenever possible.

### 4. State Government / Directorate of Medical Education

State government departments and Directorates of Medical Education may publish information concerning:

- CRMI allocation
- Internship procedures
- State counselling
- Government medical colleges
- Institutional requirements
- State-specific administrative procedures

### 5. Medical Institutions

Official institutional sources may be used for information concerning a particular college or hospital, including:

- Application procedures
- Contact information
- Institutional document requirements
- Fees
- Internship-related notices
- Administrative procedures

Institutional information should not automatically be generalized to all FMGs.

## Secondary Sources

Community sources can be useful for identifying practical problems and experiences that may not be described clearly in official documents.

Examples include:

- Reddit
- Telegram
- WhatsApp
- Medical forums
- Social media
- Personal FMG experiences

These sources should be labelled as community information.

A personal experience is evidence that something happened to one person. It is not automatically evidence that the same rule applies to everyone.

## Official Requirement vs Community Experience

The guide distinguishes between different levels of evidence.

### Official regulation

A requirement explicitly stated by an authoritative body.

### Official institutional requirement

A requirement published or communicated by a particular institution.

### Community experience

A report from an FMG or other community member describing what happened in practice.

### Interpretation

An explanation or inference made from available sources.

These categories should not be presented as interchangeable.

## Verification Dates

Information that can change should include a verification date where practical.

Example:

> Last verified: 23 September 2026

The verification date means the information was checked against the available source at that time. It does not guarantee that the information remains unchanged afterward.

## Changing Information

For time-sensitive information, prefer the most recent authoritative notice.

Examples include:

- Examination dates
- Application windows
- Fees
- CRMI seat allocations
- Registration procedures
- College requirements
- Stipend rules
- Government orders

Older information may still be useful for historical context but should not be presented as current without verification.

## Conflicting Sources

When sources conflict:

1. Record the relevant sources.
2. Check their publication dates.
3. Identify which authority issued each source.
4. Prefer the current authoritative source where applicable.
5. Explain the conflict when it is important.
6. Avoid presenting uncertain information as settled fact.

## Data Sets Must Remain Separate

Different datasets should not be merged simply because they concern the same medical college.

For example:

**MBBS seat capacity**

and

**FMG CRMI allocation**

are different datasets.

An institution may have a particular number of MBBS seats while having a different number of FMG CRMI slots.

Similarly, historical CRMI capacity should not automatically be treated as current availability.

## Links and Archived Sources

Whenever possible, link directly to the original source.

If an official document is temporarily inaccessible but an archived or mirrored copy is used for research:

- Identify it as a secondary copy.
- Retain the original official source URL where available.
- Do not imply that the mirror is the issuing authority.

## What the Guide Does Not Claim

The guide does not guarantee:

- Admission
- FMGE eligibility
- CRMI allocation
- Internship placement
- Registration approval
- Stipend payment
- Employment
- PG admission
- Acceptance by a particular institution

Readers should verify important decisions with the relevant authority or institution.

## Reporting Errors

If you find information that appears outdated or incorrect, please report it with:

- The page concerned
- The statement in question
- The correct information
- Supporting source
- Date of the source
- Date you checked it

See [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md) for contribution guidance.

## Guiding Principle

> Traceability is more important than certainty.

When the evidence is incomplete, the guide should say so.

A useful guide should distinguish between what is officially established, what is institution-specific, what has been reported by the community, and what remains uncertain.
